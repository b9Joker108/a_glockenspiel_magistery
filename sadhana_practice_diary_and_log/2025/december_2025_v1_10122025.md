Galdr is an Old Norse word for a spell, incantation, or magical chant, often associated with ancient Germanic and Norse cultures and the Germanic tribes in diaspira and pervasion. It was used for various purposes, such as healing, influencing weather, or twisting minds, and was sometimes performed with specific rites or alongside other practices like rune carving. The term could also refer to a high-pitched song used to assert one's will, with "galdr" sometimes referring to magic in general. 

**Key aspects of galdr**

* Definition: A chant, spell, or incantation. The word can be translated from Old Norse as "chant," "yell," or "crow".

**Purpose:**

* Healing: Used to fix a broken foot or for general healing purposes.
* Influencing others: Used to make someone fall in love or to twist their mind.
* Other effects: The practice was also associated with changing the weather and breaking bonds.

**Performance:**

* Vocalization: Often performed as a high-pitched song or a chant. The exact vocalization is debated, with some suggesting a crow-like sound and others a more demanding, forceful tone.
* Poetic meter: Many galdr were in rhyme or poetic meters known as gal log.
* Context: Could be performed alone or paired with other magical practices like using runes or making potions.

**Evidence:**

* Literature: Mentioned in Norse mythology, such as in the Poetic Edda and the Heimdalarhaldr poem. The Merseburg Charms are a well-known example of a continental European galdr.
* Archaeology: Runic amulets with inscriptions are believed to be related to galdr, possibly representing the written form of vocal charms.

**Modern context**

* Gaming: "Galdr table" is a term used in the video game Valheim for a crafting station.
* Contemporary practices: In modern heathenry and reconstructionist communities, the term is used to describe practices that attempt to recreate the ancient traditions, though much of the original knowledge was lost during the Christianization of Scandinavia.
