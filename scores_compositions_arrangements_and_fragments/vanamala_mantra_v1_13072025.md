You are correct: **Vanamālāmantra (वनमालामन्त्र)** is indeed a mantra addressed to the **Vanamālā**, the divine wildflower garland of Viṣṇu, and is specifically discussed in the **Padmasaṃhitā**, one of the most authoritative and widely followed texts of the Pāñcarātra tradition[1].

### Context in the Padmasaṃhitā

- The **Padmasaṃhitā**, especially in chapter 31 (Caryāpāda), presents a detailed treatment of mantras dedicated to Viṣṇu’s ornaments, including the Vanamālā.
- In this section, after narrating cosmological and mythological episodes (such as the origin of Vighnarāja/Gaṇapati), Bhagavān Viṣṇu answers Brahmā’s questions about the **mantras for his ornaments**—the crown, garland, and others.
- The **Vanamālāmantra** is one of these, specifically addressed to the Vanamālā-garland, which is not a mere decorative object but a powerful symbol and locus of divine energy in Pāñcarātra ritual and theology[1].

### Theological Significance

- In Pāñcarātra, **Viṣṇu’s ornaments** (including the Vanamālā) are not just attributes but are considered **manifestations of his cosmic functions and powers**.
- The **Vanamālā** itself represents the interconnectedness of all beings and elements, woven together in divine harmony—mirroring the garland’s composition of diverse flowers.
- Reciting the **Vanamālāmantra** is believed to invoke the protective, harmonizing, and auspicious aspects of Viṣṇu, as embodied in his garland.

### Ritual and Doctrinal Role

- The **Vanamālāmantra** is used in ritual worship (*arcana*) and forms part of the broader system of *mantras* for Viṣṇu’s divine regalia, as described in the Padmasaṃhitā.
- This reflects the Pāñcarātra’s fourfold focus: **jñāna** (knowledge), **yoga** (spiritual practice), **kriyā** (ritual action), and **caryā** (conduct), integrating theology, ritual, and cosmology[1].

It exemplifies how, in this tradition, even the Lord’s ornaments are objects of deep theological and ritual reverence, each with their own mantras and cosmological meanings[1].
